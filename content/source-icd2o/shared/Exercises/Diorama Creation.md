## Objective

Design and create a 3D diorama that replicates a room of your choosing. This could be a classroom, your dorm room, your childhood bedroom, or even a famous room from a TV show! The diorama should showcase creative design and attention to detail.

Your diorama should include essential elements that define the space (e.g., furniture, lighting, or other significant details). It should be constructed to reflect scale and proportion accurately.

Since these will not be printed, make them complex and accurate. Use any built in tools/models that are available without importing into TinkerCad
![[Pasted image 20250126211015.png]]

## What you'll need to begin

1. Ensure you are enrolled in our classroom for TinkerCAD and review the [[File submission (TinkerCAD)|file submission standards]] as well as the [[3D Printing Principles and Rules]].
2. Have a pencil, graph paper, and a ruler for sketching.
3. Choose a partner to give and receive peer feedback.

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ]  A detailed, hand-drawn plan is made for your diorama, including labeled diagrams of key elements (e.g., furniture, walls, and decor).
- [ ]  The model demonstrates an accurate use of scale and proportion based on the chosen room.
- [ ]  You consistently give and receive peer feedback, using this feedback to refine your design.
- [ ]  Your design process includes iterations and documentation of changes throughout the assignment.

## Sketch and Design

Draw your initial plan for the diorama on graph paper. Label key elements and focus on:

- [ ]  Essential features of the room (e.g., furniture, windows, and decorations with atleast 2 walls and a floor).
- [ ]  Proportions and scale (e.g., the size of a table compared to the overall room).
- [ ]  The placement of objects and their spatial relationship to one another.
- [ ]  Views from different angles (e.g., top-down, front, and side).

---

## Peer Feedback

 At the start of every class, share your sketches and progress with a peer and receive 3 pieces of **constructive feedback**. This feedback could address:

- How realistic your design looks.
- Potential challenges with printing or assembling your model.
- Suggestions for improving the overall aesthetic or function.

Include the feedback you receive in your project documentation.

> [!Remember]  
> Constructive feedback should always be:
> 
> 1. **Helpful**
> 2. **Specific**
> 3. **Kind**
